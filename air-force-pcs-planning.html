Know what each benefit is meant to cover.

PCS benefits serve different purposes. Your orders, circumstances, and the rules in effect for your move determine what applies. Use the official links to check current guidance.

Household relocation

Dislocation Allowance · DLA

A flat allowance that helps offset household relocation expenses. Grade, dependency status, and eligibility affect the amount; it may not cover everything you spend.

Ask finance: Which DLA category and rate apply, and has any of it already been paid?

DLA guidance & rates ↗

Travel to your next station

Mileage · MALT + per diem

MALT is based on authorized vehicle travel and official mileage. Per diem addresses eligible travel-day expenses. Your fuel bill and preferred travel schedule do not set these allowances.

Ask finance: Which mileage, vehicles, travelers, and authorized days should my estimate use?

Official mileage guidance ↗

Temporary lodging

TLE in CONUS · TLA OCONUS

TLE and TLA can partially offset eligible temporary-lodging expenses in different locations. They have separate conditions and approval processes. TLE and PCS travel per diem cannot both cover the same day.

Ask: Which benefit, dates, lodging arrangements, and records apply to my stay?

TLE overview ↗ TLA guidance ↗

Moving your belongings

Household goods · HHG / PPM

A government-arranged shipment and a Personally Procured Move have different arrangements. A shipment benefit is not automatically cash paid to your household. Coordinate a PPM with transportation before committing.

Ask transportation: What is authorized, what documentation is needed, and which costs would I pay?

Personal-property move guidance ↗
What about pets, childcare, vehicles, or other special costs?

Include costs you expect to pay in your budget. Ask the responsible office whether any assistance or reimbursement applies before adding it to your allowance estimate. Eligibility, limits, and documentation differ by benefit.

Review the PCS benefit cards → Official PCS entitlements overview ↗

The total cost and the cash you need are different.

An allowance estimate helps you compare the whole move. Cash planning asks whether funds are available when a specific bill must be paid.

Count each amount once.

An advance against an allowance is part of that allowance. If you expect $3,000 total and have already received $1,000, the total remains $3,000—not $4,000.

Separate borrowing from benefits.

Advance basic pay is repaid through future pay deductions. It can change when money is available, but it does not reduce the cost of your move. Ask finance about the repayment schedule.

Plan for money tied up.

A refundable housing deposit may need cash now even if you expect it back later. Track it in the cash check; include only a portion you expect to lose in your expense estimate.

Example only: $5,000 in planned expenses minus $3,500 in expected allowances leaves a $1,500 difference. Separately, $2,000 in bills due before your next payment minus $800 available now leaves a $1,200 cash gap. These figures answer different questions; do not add the two gaps together.

Where do BAH, regular pay, and the travel card fit?

Keep regular income and housing allowances in your monthly household budget. In this worksheet, compare the move’s expenses with move-specific allowances. A travel card is a payment method, not an allowance; confirm permitted use, due dates, and settlement arrangements with finance or your card program contact. Do not count unused credit as cash available.

03 PCS Planning · Section 3 of 3

Plan your shipment
and travel.

Connect your orders to the actual move. Learn how to arrange your belongings and passenger travel, then follow the process for DLA and mileage allowances.

Choose a topic, then open its steps for documents, questions, and official resources.

Move your belongings

Start with transportation counseling.

Your local transportation or personal property office helps you determine the shipment arrangements that fit your orders. In the Air Force, start with your Traffic Management Office (TMO).

1. Review your shipment options

HHG: A government-arranged household goods shipment uses an assigned moving company. PPM: A Personally Procured Move puts you in charge of arranging the move. A partial PPM combines the two.

Discuss weight limits, special items, storage, and any overseas restrictions before choosing. Unaccompanied baggage and vehicle shipment are separate arrangements when authorized.

Ask TMO: Which shipment types and weight allowances apply to my orders?

2. Apply, then confirm the schedule

Use the official moving portal and counseling instructions your transportation office provides. Military OneSource offers access to the Defense Personal Property System (DPS).

Prepare orders, contact information, a weight estimate, and proposed pickup dates. A requested date is not a confirmed appointment. Verify packing, pickup, and delivery arrangements with the assigned provider.

First or final move? Contact the transportation office before self-scheduling. Ask for help whenever your situation needs special handling.

3. Prepare for pickup or your PPM

For a government shipment, complete the pre-move survey, photograph valuable items, and review the inventory and condition descriptions. Keep documents and travel essentials out of the shipment.

For a PPM, get counseling before contracting or moving. Confirm required approvals, weight tickets, receipts, and settlement documents. Keep your PPM records separate from your passenger travel claim.

Ask TMO: What evidence do I need, and which costs or excess weight could become my responsibility?

4. Receive, inspect, and resolve issues

Keep your delivery contact details current. At delivery, compare items with the inventory and document missing or damaged property. Retain photographs and your copies of the paperwork.

A loss-or-damage notice and a formal claim are distinct steps. Ask the transportation office about applicable deadlines and the correct claims process. Report delays promptly and ask whether inconvenience-claim rules apply.

Keep three plans connected.

Your pickup date, your family's departure, and delivery at the destination may be different. Plan what you will need while belongings are in transit.

Bring to counseling: orders and amendments, proposed dates, an inventory estimate, and questions about storage or special items.

Move yourself and your family

Build the authorized travel plan.

Passenger travel moves people. Your household-goods shipment moves belongings. Coordinate both with your orders, reporting instructions, and approved leave.

1. Confirm travelers, route, and dates

Review who is authorized to travel, the authorized locations, and your reporting deadline. Identify training en route, separate dependent travel, or other special instructions.

Ask your servicing offices to distinguish authorized travel time from leave and other days. A preferred road-trip itinerary does not establish reimbursable travel days.

Ask: Which travel legs, travelers, and dates should appear in my plan?

2. Arrange tickets and travel requirements

Coordinate air travel through your passenger travel office and its designated booking process. Ask before buying or changing tickets yourself; personal preferences may affect what can be reimbursed.

For overseas travel, verify required documents, entry requirements, and any port-call instructions. Raise pet travel early: space, documentation, and transportation arrangements require their own planning.

Ask passenger travel: What is confirmed, what remains pending, and whom do I contact if the itinerary changes?

3. Travel with the records you will need

Keep accessible copies of orders, amendments, reservations, tickets, and receipts. Record actual departure and arrival dates, travel modes, and any authorized changes.

Before departure, ask finance and your travel-card program coordinator about card use, payment due dates, and required receipts. Track advances and payments already received.

Planning tip: Maintain a short daily itinerary so your eventual claim reflects the trip you actually completed.

4. Submit and review the travel claim

During in-processing, follow your servicing finance office's PCS voucher instructions. Confirm its submission system, required forms, supporting records, and deadlines.

Report advances and review the settlement for missing items or discrepancies. Keep card payments and your household cash plan current while processing is underway.

Air Force note: Direct PCS voucher questions to your servicing finance office. DFAS says it does not compute Air Force-funded PCS travel vouchers.

Use the office that owns the step.

Passenger travel: transportation arrangements and ticketing.

Finance: allowances, advances, and travel settlement.

Outbound assignments / gaining unit: orders, reporting, and local processing.

Dislocation Allowance

DLA helps with relocating your household.

DLA is a flat allowance intended to offset part of the expense of moving a household. It is separate from vehicle mileage, travel per diem, and payment for moving your belongings.

1. Confirm that your move qualifies

Ask finance to review your orders, dependent situation, and housing circumstances. Eligibility is not established merely by receiving PCS orders.

First-duty-station travel, government quarters, separation or retirement, and more than one move in a fiscal year need particular attention. Generally, DLA is limited to one payment per fiscal year unless a JTR exception applies.

Ask finance: Am I eligible, and which DLA category applies?

2. Identify the applicable rate

Use the official DTMO DLA table for the applicable effective date. Pay grade and dependency status affect the amount. Ask finance to confirm the effective date and status used for your move.

DLA is not calculated from miles driven, days on the road, or the sum of your receipts. Primary, secondary, and partial DLA have different qualifying circumstances.

Record: the rate source, applicable category, and any questions about your eligibility.

3. Ask about an advance before departure

If you need funds before the move, contact your departing installation's finance office about requesting advance DLA. Confirm its process, required documents, approvals, and expected timing.

A request is not a confirmed payment. Plan for a cash gap until funds arrive. An advance against DLA is part of the allowance; do not count it again as additional DLA after arrival.

Ask: What must I submit, and how will the advance appear in my final settlement?

4. Complete the claim and reconcile payments

Use your installation's finance process to claim or settle DLA. Provide the requested orders, amendments, and supporting information, including records of any advance.

Compare the settlement with what you already received. If eligibility, orders, or circumstances changed, ask finance whether an adjustment is needed.

Keep the distinction: DLA and an advance of basic pay are different. Ask finance about repayment terms before requesting any pay advance.

What DLA does—and does not—tell you.

It helps offset household relocation expenses. It does not promise that your total moving costs will be covered or that funds will arrive before departure.

Monetary Allowance in Lieu of Transportation

MALT is tied to authorized vehicle travel.

MALT is a mileage allowance for authorized PCS travel by privately owned vehicle (POV). It uses official distance and the applicable PCS rate, rather than reimbursing your actual fuel bill.

Official miles × applicable PCS mileage rate Basic calculation for one eligible vehicle and travel leg. Finance confirms the authorized vehicles, distance, rate, and any special rules.
1. Confirm the eligible vehicle travel

Identify the vehicles actually used, who traveled in each, and the authorized locations. Discuss separate dependent travel or multiple vehicles with finance.

The mileage amount covers authorized travelers in the vehicle. Do not multiply one vehicle's MALT by its passenger count. More than one vehicle requires review of the applicable rules.

Ask: Which vehicles and portions of my itinerary qualify?

2. Verify the distance and PCS rate

DTMO identifies the Defense Table of Official Distances as the official mileage resource. Ask finance to confirm your distance; a map application's preferred route may differ.

Use the PCS/MALT rate, not the TDY mileage rate. Check the rate applicable to your travel. Personal detours do not automatically increase payable mileage.

Bring: the authorized locations, travel dates, and questions about any indirect or mixed-mode route.

3. Understand the “Plus” in MALT-Plus

MALT is the mileage component. “Plus” refers to the applicable travel per diem for allowable travel time, helping with meals and lodging.

Per diem depends on travel circumstances, eligible travelers, and allowable days. A maximum travel-day calculation is not an automatic entitlement to that many paid days regardless of the trip actually taken.

Ask finance to separate travel days, leave, temporary lodging, and any processing or terminal delays. Do not assume one lodging rule covers every part of the move.

4. Document the trip and review settlement

Keep an itinerary showing actual dates, locations, transportation modes, and travelers. Submit the records your finance office requests and disclose advances.

Check the mileage, vehicles, per diem, and payments already received when reviewing the settlement. Raise discrepancies with the office processing your claim.

Also doing a PPM? Moving property in your vehicle and traveling in that vehicle are different processes. Ask TMO and finance which documents belong with each claim.

Four amounts to keep separate

MALT: eligible vehicle mileage.

Per diem: eligible travel-day expenses.

DLA: qualifying household relocation.

PPM settlement: your authorized personal-property move.
