The Good, The Bad, and The Air Force

Realtors play an important role in one of the biggest financial decisions most families will ever make: buying a home. A strong Realtor can be a true asset. They can help buyers understand neighborhoods, compare homes, negotiate offers, review market conditions, coordinate inspections, and avoid obvious mistakes.

For military families, a knowledgeable Realtor can also help explain VA loan expectations, commute realities, school zones, rental potential, resale concerns, and whether a home makes sense for a future PCS move.

The best Realtors are not just trying to unlock doors. They are helping families make a clear, informed, and confident decision.  But there is also a difficult truth. Real estate is a competitive field with a relatively low barrier to entry compared to the size of the financial decisions being made. That does not mean Realtors are bad or unqualified. Many are excellent professionals. But it does mean the industry has a wide range of experience, education, skill, and motivation. Some agents are true advisors. Others are primarily sales-driven. Since many Realtors earn income only when a transaction closes, their financial incentive is naturally connected to getting the buyer to the closing table. That can create a subtle conflict between “Can you buy this house?” and “Should you buy this house?”  

For military buyers, that difference matters even more. Military families often face quick turnaround timelines, short house-hunting windows, PCS orders, school-year pressure, deployment schedules, and the stress of moving an entire household across the country. Sometimes, families are forced to make housing decisions before they even arrive at the new duty location. They may rely on online photos, virtual tours, rushed phone calls, or limited local knowledge. That leaves less time to slow down, compare options, and think through the long-term impact.  

Another issue is that military income can create a false sense of safety. A steady government paycheck, BAH, BAS, and VA loan eligibility can make a home purchase look affordable on paper. But qualifying for a loan is not the same as being financially healthy. A family may have stable income and still be stretched by debt, childcare, car payments, savings gaps, emergency expenses, or the possibility of another move in three years.
