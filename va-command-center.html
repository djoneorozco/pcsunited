01 / The VA Loan Benefit

A benefit you earned.
Possibilities to explore.

VA helps eligible Veterans, service members, and surviving spouses buy, improve, or refinance a home. Explore what the benefit can do—and what to know before you use it.

The advantages ↓ Your loan path ↓ The funding fee ↓
0%Down payment possible.Keep more flexibility in your upfront budget.Tap to look closer

No down payment is usually required when the sales price does not exceed the appraised value. Your entitlement, lender, and situation matter. Closing costs and other cash needs may still apply.

No PMIOne less monthly cost.VA loans do not require mortgage insurance.Tap to look closer

There is no monthly private mortgage insurance (PMI) or FHA-style mortgage insurance premium. You still budget for principal, interest, taxes, homeowners insurance, and any HOA dues. A VA funding fee may apply.

Use againBuilt for more than once.Your benefit may support a future move, too.Tap to look closer

The benefit can be reused. Available or restored entitlement and occupancy requirements affect your next loan. Have a lender review your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), especially if you still have a VA loan.

Most VA home loans come from private lenders, with VA guaranteeing part of the loan. Benefit eligibility, lender preapproval, and final loan approval are separate steps.

If you are exempt, the funding fee is $0 regardless of down payment. Prior use solely for a manufactured home not permanently affixed may qualify for the first-use rate; ask your lender.

Who may be exempt from the funding fee?

Examples include borrowers receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability; those eligible for it but receiving retirement or active-duty pay instead; and surviving spouses receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC).

Some service members with a qualifying proposed or memorandum disability rating before closing, and active-duty Purple Heart recipients who provide evidence before closing, may also be exempt. Have the lender verify your status before you close.

Check VA’s complete exemption guidance ↗

What about refinance fees and other closing costs?

An IRRRL has a 0.50% funding fee. A cash-out refinance generally has a 2.15% first-use or 3.30% subsequent-use fee. NADL fees are 1.25% for purchase and 0.50% for refinance. Exemptions may apply.

The funding fee is separate from other closing costs and prepaid expenses. On a standard VA purchase loan, only the funding fee can be financed as a closing cost; ask your lender how the remaining costs will be covered. Get an itemized Loan Estimate to compare actual offers.

How these examples are calculated

Base loan = price − down payment. The funding fee is calculated on that base amount, then added only when financed. Principal and interest = L × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n], where L is starting loan balance, r is annual interest rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12, and n is years × 12. At 0% interest, payment = L ÷ n.

Monthly housing = principal and interest + annual property tax ÷ 12 + annual insurance ÷ 12 + monthly HOA. The interest-rate input is a note rate, not an APR. The loan is fully amortizing, with no extra payments or rate changes. Taxes, insurance, and HOA can change.

Modeled cash contribution = down payment + fee paid upfront + other costs/prepaids − applied credits. Remaining cash = contribution − deposit already paid, floored at $0. This model caps credits at other costs plus any cash-paid funding fee; it does not validate concession rules, lender-credit terms, or eligibility of particular charges. Excess credits and deposits are flagged, not presented as cash back.

Comparison rows use the same inputs except down payment. Calculations retain full precision; displays round to dollars or cents. No entitlement shortfall, appraisal gap, prorations, refund, special loan product, or local cost data is modeled. An exemption sets the fee to zero. This example does not calculate DTI, VA residual income, or qualification.

03 / Preapproval

Be ready for the lender conversation.

Start with your benefit, then your finances. A lender can help you understand a possible loan amount and what still needs to be verified.

Eligibility

Your COE

A Certificate of Eligibility confirms your VA home loan benefit and shows entitlement. It does not approve you for a mortgage.

Preapproval

A conditional review

A lender reviews financial information and may issue a letter with an amount, conditions, and expiration date. Ask what was verified.

Final approval

The completed file

The lender completes underwriting, reviews the property and appraisal, and clears outstanding conditions before closing.

What your lender will review

COE, entitlement & occupancy

Request your COE online, through a lender that can obtain it, or by mail. Service records vary: Veterans commonly need a DD214; active-duty members need a signed statement of service. Guard, Reserve, and surviving-spouse requirements differ.

Ask the lender to confirm available entitlement and funding-fee status. Full entitlement does not mean unlimited borrowing. A purchase loan is for a home you intend to occupy; discuss deployment or PCS circumstances early.

See VA’s COE instructions

Credit score & payment history

VA sets no minimum credit score, but lenders may set their own. They also review how you have handled debts and payments. Ask about the lender’s standards and discuss major financial changes before closing.

Income, debts & residual income

Bring records of stable, qualifying income and recurring obligations. Explain military pay and allowances, employment changes, and any income you expect to end or change.

Debt-to-income compares monthly debt payments, including housing, with gross monthly income. VA residual income is the amount left for family living expenses after taxes, housing, debts, and required deductions. Guidelines vary by loan size, household size, and region.

Ask your lender to explain both figures. The calculator above does not calculate residual income or decide qualification.

Savings, closing funds & reserves

Prepare statements showing funds for closing and their source. Ask whether reserves are required for your circumstances. Keep room in your own budget for moving, maintenance, and emergencies, even when no down payment is required.

Gather your starting documents.

Use this checklist for your conversation. Your lender will specify the exact documents and time periods. Checkmarks reset when you reload; nothing is uploaded.

Before relying on a letter, ask: “What is still conditional, when does this expire, and what could change my payment or cash to close?” Preapproval is not a guaranteed loan offer.

Your next step

Take your questions to a lender.

Discuss a payment you are comfortable with. Compare lenders using official Loan Estimates when available, including the rate, points, fees, and cash to close.
